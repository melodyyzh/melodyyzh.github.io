Formamidinium lead iodide (FAPbI₃) perovskites are promising materials for next-generation solar cells due to their favorable bandgap, but they suffer from phase instability — spontaneously converting from the desired photovoltaic α-phase to a non-functional δ-phase under ambient conditions.

During my undergraduate research in the Dou Group at Purdue University, I worked on interface engineering strategies to address this challenge:

- Modified perovskite surfaces using synthesized ligands to passivate defects and kinetically trap the photovoltaic crystal phase
- Incorporated amine-thiol chemistry in the perovskite layer, achieving a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 20.1% with improved stability under ambient conditions
- Streamlined the data analysis pipeline for photovoltaic device characterization
