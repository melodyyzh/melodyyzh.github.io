I am a Ph.D. candidate in Chemical Engineering and Scientific Computing at the University of Michigan, advised by Professors [Rebecca K. Lindsey](https://lindseylab.engin.umich.edu/) and [Sharon C. Glotzer](https://glotzerlab.engin.umich.edu/home/). My research focuses on developing physics-informed machine-learned interaction models for coarse-grained nanomaterials that encode chemical and physical design parameters, enabling efficient inverse design and discovery of self-assembling materials. I am also developing generalized machine-learned interaction models for anisotropic nanoparticles, where relative position and orientation govern collective assembly behavior.

More broadly, I am passionate about integrating AI with scientific computing to accelerate scientific discovery. My interests span machine learning, molecular simulation, high-performance computing, and materials informatics, and I enjoy applying these tools to real-world challenges, including developing AI-enabled modeling and data infrastructure during my internship at Micron Technology.

Beyond my research, I am a writer for MRS Bulletin Materials News, where I interview researchers and corresponding authors about their latest discoveries and write research highlights that make cutting-edge materials science accessible to a broad audience.

I enjoy staying active through boxing, snowboarding, and running. I also enjoy landscape and automotive photography, photo and video editing, and gaming.
